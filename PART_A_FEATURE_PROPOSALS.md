# Part A - Coy App Exploration and Feature Proposals

## App Exploration

I explored Coy's main onboarding and crush-management experience, including phone OTP login, adding a crush, email verification, sharing an invite link, and viewing the extra-slots screen.

The most important part of Coy's experience is privacy. Users should feel comfortable expressing interest without revealing their identity to someone who has not shown mutual interest.

Based on this experience, I suggest the following two features.

## Feature 1: Private Crush Management

### What should this feature do?

Give users a private screen to manage the crushes they have added.

Users should be able to:
- Review their own added crushes.
- Correct an accidentally entered name or email.
- Withdraw a crush if they change their mind.
- See a clear confirmation before making changes.

These actions must not reveal who has added the user as a crush.

### Why is this feature required?

Adding a crush is a personal and sensitive action. Sometimes a user might enter the wrong details or change their mind.

Giving users more control can reduce mistakes, improve trust, and make the experience feel safer.

Privacy must remain the priority. Editing or removing a crush should not reveal another person's interest.

## Feature 2: Invitation and Extra-Slot Progress Tracker

### What should this feature do?

Add a simple dashboard explaining invitation progress and available crush slots.

The dashboard could show:
- The user's available crush slots.
- How to earn additional slots through referrals.
- Which invitations have been accepted, when such tracking is available.
- Whether an invitation has expired.
- Clear explanations of any conditions for unlocking slots.

It should avoid exposing sensitive information about other users.

### Why is this feature required?

Coy includes invite links and an extra-slots feature.

Users may want to know how the referral process works and what they need to do to unlock another slot.

A clear progress tracker could reduce confusion, make the feature easier to understand, and encourage users to continue using Coy.

## Conclusion

Both features aim to improve the user experience while protecting Coy's central privacy promise.

The first proposal gives users more control over sensitive actions. The second makes invitations and additional slots easier to understand.

These are product improvement proposals based on my exploration, not claims that equivalent features are definitely absent from Coy today.
